               transfer (via SCP/SFTP) to this machine.
Security risk: LOW–MEDIUM. SSH is encrypted by design, so traffic
               interception isn't the main concern. Real-world risk
               comes from:
                 - Password-based authentication being enabled, which
                   is brute-forceable over time.
                 - The service being reachable from untrusted networks
                   (e.g., public Wi-Fi, the open internet) rather than
                   just the home LAN.
                 - Root login being permitted directly over SSH.
               OpenSSH 10.2p1 is a recent version, so no known
               unpatched CVEs are expected at time of writing.
Recommendation: Use SSH key-based authentication instead of passwords,
               disable root login (PermitRootLogin no), keep the
               package updated via apt, and avoid exposing port 22 to
               the public internet without a firewall/VPN in front of it.
